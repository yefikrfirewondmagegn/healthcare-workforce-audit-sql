# HEALTHCARE WORKFORCE DATA ANALYSIS
## Statistical Findings and Business Interpretation

 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes healthcare workforce data to examine employee burnout patterns, working hours, and differences across hospital departments.

The analysis was conducted using RStudio.

Dataset overview:
- Total observations: 120 healthcare workers
- Total variables: 12
- Professions: Nurses, Doctors, Administrators, and Laboratory Staff
- Departments: Emergency, ICU, Neurology, Oncology, and Pediatrics

 2. Workforce Composition

The dataset contains:
- Nurses: 51 (42.5%)
- Doctors: 43 (35.8%)
- Administrators: 13 (10.8%)
- Laboratory Staff: 13 (10.8%)

This provides a workforce sample representing four different professional groups.

 3. Burnout Score Analysis

The descriptive statistical analysis produced the following results:

- Average burnout score: 6.28
- Median burnout score: 6.20
- Minimum score: 3.10
- Maximum score: 9.70

The average burnout score was approximately 6.28. Scores varied across the workforce, indicating differences in reported burnout levels.

 4. Burnout Category Analysis

Based on the project's defined burnout categories:

- Higher burnout: 45 workers (37.5%)
- Moderate burnout: 55 workers (45.8%)
- Lower burnout: 20 workers (16.7%)

The moderate category represented the largest proportion of the workforce. Meanwhile, 37.5% of workers were classified in the higher burnout category.

These categories are project-defined classifications and should not be interpreted as clinical diagnoses.

 5. Working Hours and Burnout Relationship

A Pearson correlation test was conducted to investigate the relationship between weekly working hours and burnout scores.

Results:
- Correlation coefficient: 0.0027
- P-value: 0.9764
- Significance level: 0.05

Interpretation:

The analysis identified virtually no linear correlation between weekly working hours and burnout scores.

The p-value exceeded the 0.05 significance threshold, indicating that the observed relationship was not statistically significant.

This suggests that working hours alone may not adequately explain differences in burnout scores within this dataset.

 6. Departmental Burnout Comparison

A one-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was conducted to examine whether average burnout scores differed across the five departments.

Results:
- F-statistic: 0.993
- P-value: 0.415
- Significance level: 0.05

Interpretation:

The analysis did not identify statistically significant differences in average burnout scores across the five departments.

The observed departmental differences were not statistically significant at the 5% significance level.

 7. Key Findings

1. Moderate burnout was the most common project-defined category, representing 45.8% of workers.
2. Approximately 37.5% of workers were classified in the higher burnout category.
3. Weekly working hours showed virtually no linear correlation with burnout scores.
4. No statistically significant differences in average burnout scores were identified across departments.

 8. Business Implications

The findings highlight the importance of monitoring employee well-being across healthcare departments and professional groups.

Healthcare organizations could investigate additional factors such as workload, staffing levels, working conditions, management support, and employee satisfaction to better understand burnout.

The results suggest that relying exclusively on working hours or departmental differences may provide an incomplete picture of employee burnout.

 9. Limitations

- The dataset contains 120 observations and may not represent the entire healthcare workforce.
- Burnout categories were defined for this project and are not clinical classifications.
- Correlation does not establish causation.
- The analysis examines selected variables and does not account for every potential factor associated with burnout.

 10. Tools Used

- MySQL Workbench: Data cleaning, standardization, validation, and SQL analysis.
- RStudio: Statistical analysis, hypothesis testing, and data visualization.
- R: Descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and one-way ANOVA.

Project conclusion:

This project demonstrates an end-to-end data analysis workflow, from raw workforce data cleaning and validation to statistical investigation, visualization, and communicating findings through business-oriented interpretations.
